Revisiting, connecting, and processing notes isn't wasted time; it's the essence of meaningful intellectual work that transforms 
information into insight.
When you move back through your notes and ask "what is this connecting to?" or explore the "maybe's" and possibilities, you're 
engaging in the core activity that transforms scattered information into coherent understanding.

The real value in note-taking emerges not from the initial capture of information, but from the ongoing processing. Each time 
you revisit your notes, you're cleaning them up, maintaining them, and most importantly, drawing new creative connections. This iterative 
engagement is where breakthrough insights typically occur.

Without active processing, you risk creating a massive database filled with information that was only valuable in the moment of capture. 
These notes become digital clutter rather than intellectual assets. 

### Purpose-Driven Tags
Traditional tagging approaches focus on categorization; asking "what keyword fits this note?" or "what category does this belong to?" 
This backward-looking approach limits the future utility of your notes. Instead, effective tagging requires forward-thinking questions 
that consider future discovery and application.

Rather than asking what category a note fits into, ask yourself: "In which circumstances will I want to stumble upon this note in the future?" 
This shifts your perspective from filing information away to creating pathways for future rediscovery. Consider the contexts, projects, or 
problems where this particular insight might prove valuable.
