Atomic notes represent a fundamental principle in effective knowledge management: each note should contain one distinct, 
well-defined idea with its own clear title and explanation. 
Each note should capture a single idea or concept, complete with a descriptive title that immediately communicates its content. 

An effective atomic note consists of 3 essential elements: 
- A distinctive title that clearly identifies the core idea: don't use titles that are broad, instead of creating a broad note 
titled "Vitamin D," one might create individual notes with titles like "Vitamin D can prevent kidney stones" and "Vitamin D is generated by sunlight".
- A focused explanation that fully develops that idea without wandering into related topics. This is an atomic note, please don't turn this 
into an academic paper, it's supposed to be short and expand to the initial idea from the title.
- Supporting organizational elements like dates or tags that help contextualize and retrieve the information later.

When notes become too complex or lengthy, they lose their flexibility and become rigid, monolithic blocks of information. Complex notes resist 
decomposition and less reusable making it nearly impossible to extract specific insights or apply them in different contexts. 

Atomicity directly enables creativity by maintaining the fluidity of ideas. It needs to be compact! 
Small, focused notes can be easily recombined, cross-referenced, and applied in unexpected ways. They serve as building blocks that can be 
assembled into larger concepts while retaining their individual identity & utility. 

A well-crafted atomic note can seamlessly integrate into multiple projects, discussions, or thought processes because it carries a complete, 
self-contained idea. Insights developed in one area can naturally migrate to inform thinking in entirely different domains.
